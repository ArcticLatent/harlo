Review

Read what the agent did before you keep it

  • Diffs side by side or inline, with syntax highlighting.
  • Undo a turn: put back the files as they were before it, in every repository it touched.
  • Commit, push and open a pull request from the Changes panel, then follow its CI: GitHub, GitLab and Forgejo, your own server too. When checks fail, hand the failure to the agent.

Why native

Built like a desktop app, because it is one

Many AI agent apps are web pages in an Electron window. harlo is Rust and Qt from top to bottom.

Its window is drawn about a third of a second after it starts, and idle it uses 0.0–0.2% of one core. Measured on 12 November 2026: the release build, started three times (warm), with six sessions in the sidebar, on a Ryzen 9 9900X with an RTX 5090, under KDE Plasma (Wayland). The time is from launch to the first full frame; the CPU is over 10 seconds of doing nothing.

A Rust core
Agent streams, transcripts, diffs, git and search run in compiled Rust, on threads of their own. Long sessions don't slow the window down.
A Qt Quick interface
Drawn by your GPU with Qt 6, the toolkit behind KDE Plasma. No HTML, no JavaScript framework, no bundled Chromium.
At home on Linux
Native windows on Wayland and X11, your system's notifications, input methods and file dialogs, a drop-down window, and packages for your distribution.

A wiki of your code

Your agents look it up first

  • Written from the code: Create wiki has your agent write the repository's architecture, workflows and the reasons behind them, every fact tied to the lines it came from (with OpenWiki, which comes with harlo).
  • Read in sections: your agents search it and read only the parts they need, then check them in the code.
  • The Wiki panel: pages with their diagrams and sources, search, and a graph of how the pages link.
  • Kept current: it says how far behind the code it is, and Update rewrites only the pages whose code changed.

Plans and diagrams

Bigger work, one step at a time

  • A plan you can edit: the agent writes the steps and works through them; reorder, change or skip any of them.
  • Run with helpers: a session per step. Steps that don't depend on each other run at once, each in a worktree, a checkout of its own, and another agent reviews each one.
  • Choices as cards with their pros and cons and a Choose button, diagrams drawn as pictures, and mockups of a UI change, before and after.

On your iPhone

Away from your desk, still in the loop

  • Answer questions and approvals from a notification, even with the app closed.
  • Live Activities on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island while a session works, and a home screen widget.
  • Start sessions on any of your machines, with templates, photos and dictation.
  • Read the wiki and follow the plan: a project's pages and search, a plan's steps and its helpers.
  • End-to-end encrypted between the phone and your computer: on your Wi-Fi, over Tailscale, or through harlo's relay, which can't read what it passes on.

On your computer

And everything around it

Each agent runs with your own login and settings, and does more than code: release notes, research, reviews.

Two agents, one chat
Harlo with your ChatGPT plan, an OpenAI key or your own models, and Claude Code with your Claude login: your sessions show up, ready to carry on.
Your other machines
Add a machine you reach over SSH and work there as you would here: its terminal, files, changes and models.
Your own models
llama.cpp, Ollama, vLLM and more, set up for your graphics card with a model that fits, here or on a GPU box.
Limits and spending
Your Claude and ChatGPT plans' limits as they change, and a spending limit per session or per day.

Also: dictation on your computer, search across every conversation, pictures in the chat, a terminal, files and templates, and a log of everything an agent did.
